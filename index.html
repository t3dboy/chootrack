The railway is
stranger than that.

A week of riding real trains and reading real boards, and most of what follows came from something being wrong in a way no test had thought to ask about. Every picture below is the app drawing itself: these are the snapshots its own test suite renders and compares on every run, so a screenshot here cannot quietly stop matching the app.

SPLITS

A train that splits in half is still one train

The 08:35 from Victoria divides at Horsham and the back half carries on to Billingshurst. Every app calls that a change, because two ends of one train carry two identities in the data. ChooTrack calls it what the guard calls it: one journey, one spine, a marker at the split you can tap. The instruction is to stay seated, and now the app says so, and keeps tracking you straight through the divide.

ARRIVALS

“When do I actually get there?”

Filter a board to the trains that call where you are going and each row answers the real question: the arrival time, and how long it takes. The loop-line service that gets there fifty minutes later no longer looks identical to the fast one.

CANCELLED

Cancelled, and a way out

The spine turns coral, the operator's own reason appears when they gave one, and one tap finds the next train doing the same job. The board and the service screen can no longer contradict each other either, which they could when the two feeds disagreed.

TRAMS

Every tram in Britain, drawn line by line

Nine networks and 723 stops: the Underground and DLR, Manchester's Metrolink, the Sheffield Supertram, Nottingham, the West Midlands, Blackpool's seafront, Croydon, the Glasgow Subway, the Tyne and Wear Metro and the Edinburgh trams. Ask for Sheffield to Rotherham Parkgate and you get the Blue line to Fitzalan Square and then the tram-train, each in its own colour, every stop named, on a map. Half of these places have no railway station at all: Salford Quays, Leith, the Trafford Centre, Edinburgh Airport. Until now the app could not take you to any of them.

TUBE

Modelled, not looked up

Cross-city hops used to be a table of cautious guesses with one blanket number each. The networks are modelled now, so a change opens up into the lines you actually ride, in their own colours, with the walk between them stated when there is one.

REWIND

The scrubber, at half the height

Drag the tape and time slides under the needle. Going back warms to coral, going forward cools to teal, and both deepen the further you travel, so you can feel how far you are from now without reading a number. The board gets the space back.

And the quiet ones: thirty five stations that had no departures at all, because of a broken code map. Sleepers deleted before they reached you. Cardiff listing one train twice, going to two different places. A walk between two Tube stations that claimed one minute whatever the distance. Trams and Tubes are new; most of this list is the app being less wrong, which is the part you only find by riding the trains.

Transparency · under the bonnet

How ChooTrack
actually works.

No mystery meat. This is the whole machine: what leaves your phone (almost nothing), where the train data comes from (three official feeds), and how updates reach your Lock Screen without the app even being open.

Boards · National Rail Live progress · Realtime Trains Timetable · Network Rail, nightly Push · Apple APNs Your requests · HTTPS

* Your location leaves your phone only when you tap “Guess my train”, and only to ChooTrack’s own server, never to the rail feeds.

01

One server, not many APIs

The app never talks to rail APIs directly. Every request goes to ChooTrack’s own backend, and the only things it ever sends are the journey you’re tracking, plus a single location fix if you use Guess my train.

02

Three official sources

Boards come from National Rail Enquiries (LDBWS, licensed via the Rail Data Marketplace). Live running, where a train actually is, its delays and platforms, comes from Realtime Trains. The timetable comes from Network Rail itself.

03

A timetable that sleeps at night

Network Rail’s national SCHEDULE feed, the full GB timetable, is refreshed on the server every night at around 3am. By morning it has been chopped into the quarter of a million hops that power the journey planner just below.

04

A polite API citizen

The backend caches hard, a station’s board is reused for around 25 seconds, and keeps a global cap on calls to the upstream feeds, so ChooTrack stays a considerate consumer of the industry’s data.

05

Push, not polling

Track a train and the server watches it for you, pushing every change to your Lock Screen Live Activity through Apple’s push service. The app doesn’t need to be open, or even running.

06

Delay chips

“On time”, “+4 late”, platform changes and cancellations come from comparing the booked timetable against the expected and actual times in the live feeds, the same arithmetic a station announcer does.

The journey planner

No direct train?
It finds the way.

Ask for Billingshurst to Edinburgh and no single train will take you. The planner stitches one anyway: Southern to Three Bridges, Thameslink to Blackfriars, the Tube to King's Cross (real lines, real stops, priced by how often the trains run), then LNER all the way north. And where the walk beats the ride, it says so: Blackfriars to City Thameslink is six minutes on foot, and ChooTrack won't send you underground for it. Where two Tube lines meet at different stations it says that too, with the walk and its length, and where the change stays inside one building it says follow the signs instead, because those are different instructions. Here is the whole machine, from the nightly build to the journey it hands you.

Nightly · ~3am Whole GB timetable Network Rail's SCHEDULE feed, about a million lines, plus the codes that map raw locations to stations.
Parse + resolve ~177,000 trains Passenger and replacement services over a 27-day window. Short-term changes and cancellations resolved so each date is what will really run.
Held in memory Millions of hops Every train exploded into station-to-station hops, sorted by departure. Station change times join here, and cross-city transfers come from ChooTrack's own Tube, Metro and Tram model: 723 stops across nine networks, every tram and light rail system in Britain, priced by how often the trains run.
Snapshot · ~7 MB Restart? Reloads in seconds Each build writes a compact snapshot to disk. Any restart or update loads it instantly. NO RE-INGEST Only the nightly refresh does the heavy build again.
One Connection Scan · sweeps the whole day once · answers in milliseconds · 0 external calls

Changes you can make

A change is only offered when you can genuinely make it: a 5 minute minimum by default, longer at the big stations, and 15 minutes at London terminals like King's Cross.

TUBE

Cross-London, modelled

Cross-city hops are not a lookup table of cautious guesses any more. ChooTrack models the networks themselves, station by station and line by line, and routes across them like the railway: 723 stops across the Underground and DLR, Manchester's Metrolink, the Sheffield Supertram, Nottingham, the West Midlands, Blackpool, Croydon, the Glasgow Subway, the Tyne and Wear Metro and the Edinburgh trams. A transfer opens up into the lines you actually ride, and is never counted as a change.
